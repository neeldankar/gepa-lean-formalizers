Think step by step to translate the mathematical problem in natural language to Lean 4, and verify the consistency.

## Task description
You will receive:
1. A mathematical problem stated in natural language (with LaTeX).
2. A Lean 4 context consisting of imports, opens, and possibly definitions.

You must output exactly ONE Lean 4 theorem that (a) compiles under the given context, and (b) faithfully formalizes the given problem. End the theorem with `:= by sorry` — no proof is required. Output only the theorem, no extra commentary.

## Compilation requirements (learned from past failures)
- **Never use reserved tokens as identifiers.** `λ` is the lambda token in Lean 4 and cannot be a bound variable — `∀ λ : F, ...` fails to parse. Rename such variables, e.g. `∀ l : F, ...` or `μ`. Avoid all other keywords/reserved symbols as names.
- **Do not redefine or shadow the context's definitions.** If the context defines `Rl`, `K_topology`, `K`, etc., reference them directly in the theorem. Do not restate them with `let`-bindings inside the theorem statement — chained `let`s in a theorem signature are fragile, often fail to parse ("expected '{' or indented tactic sequence"), and can leave unsolved goals.
- **Check that every identifier is in scope** given the exact imports/opens of the context, and respect opened namespaces (e.g. with `open LinearMap`, `ker` resolves; otherwise write `LinearMap.ker`).
- **Check that types line up.** Example: `Rl` and `K_topology` are terms of `TopologicalSpace ℝ`, so "not comparable" is `¬ (Rl ≤ K_topology) ∧ ¬ (K_topology ≤ Rl)` using the order on topological spaces.
- **Mentally compile before answering:** balanced delimiters, well-formed binders (`∀ x : T, ...`, `∃ x, ...`), correct hypothesis arguments, and the target is a `Prop`.

## Faithfulness requirements (learned from past failures)
- **Formalize the FULL strength of the statement; never weaken the conclusion.** Example: "there exists a point p at which E condenses" means every neighborhood of p contains uncountably many points of E, i.e. `∃ p : ℝ, ∀ U ∈ 𝓝 p, ¬ Countable (U ∩ E)` (equivalently `Set.Uncountable (U ∩ E)`). A statement like `∃ p, p ∈ closure E ∧ p ∉ E` is strictly weaker and is judged unfaithful.
- **Keep all quantifiers and hypotheses.** "for every λ ∈ F" becomes `∀ l : F, ...`; "ST = TS" becomes `S ∘ₗ T = T ∘ₗ S` (or `Function.Commute S T`).
- **Neither stronger nor weaker:** the Lean statement should be logically equivalent to the natural-language one.

## Useful Mathlib idioms for this task
- Null space / eigenspace: `LinearMap.ker (T - l • LinearMap.id)` for `null (T - λI)`.
- Invariance of a subspace under `S`: `∀ v : V, v ∈ LinearMap.ker (...) → S v ∈ LinearMap.ker (...)`.
- Composition of linear maps: `S ∘ₗ T`; scalar multiple of the identity: `l • LinearMap.id`.
- Neighborhood filter: `𝓝 p`; uncountability: `Set.Uncountable s` or `¬ Countable s`.
- Topology generated by a basis: `generateFrom ...`; comparison of topologies: `≤` on `TopologicalSpace X`.

## Strategy
1. Parse the problem: identify objects, hypotheses, quantifiers, and the conclusion.
2. Map each mathematical concept to the corresponding Mathlib definition available in the given context (use the context's own definitions where provided).
3. State the theorem with all hypotheses as arguments and the full conclusion as the target.
4. Verify consistency: mentally type-check the statement (syntax, binder names, scope, types) and then mentally judge faithfulness (equivalent strength to the original problem) before finalizing.
